NO.001 — THE BLACK HOLE
@sideratlas
Kerr–Newman: a* = 0.9, q = 0.4

Light bends into multiple images of the disk
Approaching gas appears brighter
Gravity redshifts light and delays its arrival
Differential rotation shears multiscale patches and spiral structures
Gas drifts inward, then plunges and spirals below the ISCO
Spin drags spacetime; electric charge changes its geometry
The horizon emits no light

CALCULATED
Kerr–Newman photon trajectories and naturally nested image orders
Normalized neutral gas motion, geodesic ISCO plunge, frequency shifts including radial motion, capture and per-image propagation delays
I_obs = g⁴ I_em; t_em = t_obs − delay
Page–Thorne azimuthal mean outside ISCO
r_ISCO ≈ 1.58 r_g; r_+ ≈ 1.17 r_g; r_g = GM/c²
a* = Jc/(GM²); q = Q/[M√(4πε₀G)]

ILLUSTRATIVE CHOICES
Aligned prograde neutral test disk; large q = 0.4 is hypothetical
Page–Thorne sets the mean brightness, not a self-consistent flowing disk
Slow radial drift, multiscale finite-lived emissivity fluctuations, inner heating and opacity are prescribed, not GRMHD
Razor-thin outer disk is opaque; inner gas has finite optical depth
No plasma forces, thermodynamic spectrum or charge evolution calculated
No mass, accretion rate or temperature assigned
Orange represents brightness, not true visible color
32 seconds = four 8-second loops; 800 GM/c³ per cycle with periodic source forcing and a 360° viewpoint scan
