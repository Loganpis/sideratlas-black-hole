NO.001 — THE BLACK HOLE
GRAVITY TYPE · @atlastro

It bends light into several views of one disk. Its spin drags spacetime; its gravity and orbiting gas shift the spectrum. The approaching side brightens. Its opaque foreground disk hides some farther images.

These colors come from a thermal spectrum, approximate atmosphere corrections, relativistic transfer and CIE human-color matching—not an orange filter. They describe this model, not a universal black-hole color. The dark center is not a direct outline of its horizon.

Model: Kerr spin a*=0.9; 10¹⁰ solar masses; ηṀc²/L_Edd=0.05; disk 2.321–500 GM/c². Mass and accretion rate differ from the earlier example. This 8-second loop scans 80°–86° viewing angles of a steady thin disk; it does not show evolving gas or a full GRMHD simulation.

Iν,obs = Υ(μ) f_col⁻⁴ Bν(ν, g f_col T_eff).

The main version adds subtle camera diffraction: a 20 m circular aperture, 550 nm reference kernel applied equally to RGB. That approximation is a camera effect, not a glow emitted by the hole. The ideal-optics version removes it. Both use one fixed exposure.
