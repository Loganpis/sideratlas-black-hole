NO.001 — THE BLACK HOLE
GRAVITY TYPE · @atlastro

One flat disk. Several impossible-looking views.

Its gravity bends light around it, revealing the disk's far side. Its orbiting gas makes the approaching side brighter. It traps light inside its horizon; the dark image is not a direct outline of that horizon.

Calculated light paths, no painted glow: this is a non-spinning black hole with a steady relativistic thin disk. The grayscale Reel shows energy summed over all frequencies: I_obs = g⁴F/π. The optional color version integrates its visible thermal spectrum: T_obs = gT_eff.

Model: 10⁸ solar masses, disk 6–40 GM/c², ηṀc²/L_Edd = 0.1. The eight-second loop scans viewing angles 78°–87°; it does not show eight seconds of evolving gas. This is an idealized thin-disk calculation, not a full GRMHD simulation. No spin, corona, jets or turbulent flow are modeled.

Studies used:
• Page & Thorne (1974), “Disk-Accretion onto a Black Hole. I. Time-Averaged Structure of Accretion Disk” — disk flux. doi.org/10.1086/152990
• Luminet (1979), “Image of a spherical black hole with thin accretion disk” — lensing and brightness. A&A 75, 228–235; articles.adsabs.harvard.edu/pdf/1979A%26A....75..228L
• Müller & Frauendiener (2012), “Interactive visualization of a thin disc around a Schwarzschild black hole” — disk-rendering equations. arxiv.org/abs/1206.4259
• James et al. (2015), “Gravitational Lensing by Spinning Black Holes in Astrophysics, and in the Movie Interstellar” — appearance/transfer reference, not its Kerr/DNGR code. arxiv.org/abs/1502.03808
• Bozza (2002), “Gravitational lensing in the strong field limit” — numerical validation. arxiv.org/abs/gr-qc/0208075

Color data: CIE (2019), “Colour-matching functions of CIE 1931 standard colorimetric observer,” doi.org/10.25039/CIE.DS.xvudnb9b. Display conversion: ICC, “How to interpret the sRGB color space,” registry.color.org/rgb-registry/files/sRGB.pdf.
