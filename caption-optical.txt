NO.001 — THE BLACK HOLE
GRAVITY TYPE · @atlastro

It bends light into several images of its disk. Its spin drags spacetime. Its gravity and orbiting gas shift the spectrum; the approaching side brightens. Its opaque foreground disk hides some farther images.

Its visible color comes from calculated thermal spectra, approximate atmosphere corrections, relativistic transfer and CIE human-color matching. These colors describe this model. The dark center is not its horizon's direct outline.

Kerr spin a*=0.9; 10¹⁰ solar masses; ηṀc²/L_Edd=0.05; thin disk 2.321–500 GM/c². This 8-second loop scans 85°–88° views of a steady disk, not evolving gas.

Iν,obs = Υ(μ) f_col⁻⁴ Bν(ν, g f_col T_eff).

New optics: 48 visible wavelengths, each diffracted before CIE color integration. The hypothetical 10 m circular aperture spreads red light more than blue. This camera effect is separate from gravitational lensing. The ideal-optics companion removes diffraction. Both share fixed exposure and a screen-contrast curve. Finite disk thickness, turbulence and jets are omitted; this is not GRMHD.
